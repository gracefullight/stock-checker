# Stock rules, SEC filings and calendar events on the S&P 500 — 2026-11-27

The [entry research](entry-research-2026-11-26.md) ended on a data limit: the
configured universe is today's ticker list, so no stock rule could be validated
on it. This run removes most of that limit, repeats the three strongest stock
rules, and then adds two kinds of information that are not price or volume:
insider purchases and earnings announcement dates, both from SEC filings, and
three calendar events on the index itself. **One rule passed its development
period, the year after a midterm election, and it did not clear the bar on the
held-out elections. No rule is validated.** Nothing here changes the shipped
configuration.

## Dataset

- **Membership**: S&P 500 constituent snapshots at each change, from
  [fja05680/sp500](https://github.com/fja05680/sp500) (`S&P 500 Historical
  Components & Changes (Updated).csv`). 977 tickers were members on at least one
  session since 2005.
- **Prices**: adjusted daily bars from Yahoo for tickers it still serves, and
  from Tiingo for delisted ones (208 symbols of the free plan's 500 a month).
  Per ticker, the source that prices more of its member-days is used.
- **Renames**: a member listed under an old symbol is priced from its current
  one. Twenty renames were found from Wikipedia's "Date added" column: when a
  current member joined the index before its symbol first appears, the symbol
  that disappears that day is its predecessor, unless it kept trading or was
  delisted in its own right (`renames.mjs`; two same-day pairs are set by hand).
- **Delistings**: a position whose series ends inside the hold is sold at its
  last close.

Fingerprint of the price files, membership, rename map and Wikipedia table:
`5f383633…`. The data is kept under `data/strategy-validation/sp500-pit-20261127`
and is not published.

Members with the bars a rule needs (252 sessions of history and the next open),
by formation year:

| 2006 | 2008 | 2010 | 2012 | 2014 | 2016 | 2018 | 2020 | 2022 | 2024 | 2026 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 70% | 79% | 85% | 87% | 90% | 92% | 95% | 97% | 98% | 99% | 99% |

89.8% of all member-days since 2005 are priced. The rest are 71 symbols later
reused by another listed company (the earlier member cannot be told apart),
121 that Tiingo does not list, and 32 it lists only in part. Every member
Tiingo can price has been fetched. The gap is
largest before 2010 and consists of delisted names, so a rule that buys losers
is still flattered in the early years.

## Protocol

Written into `weekly.mjs` before any return from this dataset was read.

- Every fifth session, rank the members of that date and buy the top decile at
  the next open; sell at the fifth close; 10 bps per round trip.
- Three rules: 12-1 month momentum, five-session losers, and the mean of the
  two percentile ranks (the product's leader-pullback thesis).
- Benchmarks over the same window and cost: SPY, and all ranked members in
  equal weight.
- DEV is formation dates 2006 through 2015; TEST is 2016 onward and is printed
  only for a rule that passes DEV.
- Pass: mean weekly excess over SPY and over the members both have a 95%
  bootstrap interval above zero (weeks resampled 2,000 times, seed 20261127).
  The same excess with each position clamped to ±50% must agree in sign.
- The first read used 200 Tiingo symbols; the last eight queued ones (0.2
  points of member-days) were added afterwards. The tables below are from the
  complete dataset, and no figure moved by more than 0.05 points.

## Result (DEV, 504 weeks)

| Rule | Weekly mean net | Excess vs SPY | Excess vs members | Clamped (SPY / members) | Weeks beating SPY |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12-1 month momentum | −0.01% | −0.04% (−0.18…+0.10) | −0.08% (−0.24…+0.06) | −0.04% / −0.08% | 52% |
| Five-session losers | +0.18% | +0.15% (−0.05…+0.37) | +0.10% (−0.07…+0.28) | +0.12% / +0.08% | 52% |
| Leader pullback (mean rank) | +0.12% | +0.08% (−0.06…+0.21) | +0.04% (−0.10…+0.17) | +0.08% / +0.05% | 55% |

SPY earns +0.03% and the members +0.08% a week on the same terms. Every
interval includes zero. Momentum is negative; the loser rule has the largest
point estimate and is the one the missing delisted names flatter. TEST was not
run for any rule.

## Insider purchases (`insider.mjs`, DEV only)

Source: the SEC's quarterly Insider Transactions Data Sets, 2006 Q1 to 2026 Q1
(1,211,337 open-market purchase rows on Form 4). Filings are matched to index
members by the issuer's CIK; `cik-map.mjs` builds the ticker-to-CIK table from
the symbol each issuer's filers reported at the time (925 of 977 member tickers).

Registered before any event's return was read: purchases by officers or
directors filed in a trailing 30-day window; enter at the open after the
filing date on which the rule first holds, for a member of that date; hold 60
sessions; intervals resample entry quarters.

| Rule | Trades | Win rate (SPY) | Mean net | Excess vs SPY | Excess vs members |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| At least 2 buyers and $200,000 | 559 | 61.0% (62.4%) | +3.26% | +2.07% (−1.40…+6.90) | +1.03% (−1.24…+3.87) |
| At least $500,000, any buyers | 702 | 58.7% (62.8%) | +3.04% | +2.00% (−0.83…+5.93) | +1.24% (−0.75…+3.71) |

The point estimates come from one episode: 2008–2009 contributes +7.5% over
SPY, while 2010–2012 is −0.2% and 2013–2015 is −1.5% for the two-buyer rule.
Neither interval leaves zero. TEST was not run.

The first run matched filings by the symbol text and read free text such as
"N/A" as the tickers N and A. Matching by CIK removed 35 of 593 two-buyer
trades and moved the excess by 0.05 points; the conclusion is the same.

## Earnings announcement windows (`earnings.mjs`, DEV only)

Source: 66,581 dates of 8-K filings carrying item 2.02 for 961 CIKs, from the
EDGAR submissions API. Only the spacing of the dates was inspected before the
rule was fixed: 68% of announcements fall within three calendar days of the
prior year's date plus 364 days, and 91% within seven.

Registered: the expected date is a past announcement plus 364 days, so it is
known a year ahead; hold from five sessions before it to five sessions after
(eleven sessions) for a member of the entry date; intervals resample entry
quarters.

| Period | Windows | Win rate (SPY) | Mean net | Excess vs SPY | Excess vs members |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2006–2015 | 15,954 | 54.7% (62.5%) | +0.70% | +0.25% (−0.03…+0.59) | +0.09% (−0.03…+0.22) |
| 2006–2007 | 2,707 | 51.5% (59.2%) | −0.09% | −0.09% | +0.06% |
| 2008–2009 | 3,111 | 57.4% (63.0%) | +1.68% | +1.06% | +0.48% |
| 2010–2012 | 4,938 | 51.6% (60.2%) | +0.16% | +0.09% | −0.10% |
| 2013–2015 | 5,198 | 57.6% (66.0%) | +1.03% | +0.09% | +0.04% |

It comes close and still fails: both intervals reach
below zero, the excess over the members is nine hundredths of a percent per
window, and outside 2008–2009 it is indistinguishable from nothing. TEST was
not run.

## Calendar events on the index (`events.mjs`)

These are statements about when the index itself does well, so the benchmark
is the same hold entered on any day of the same period.

Registered before any return around the events was read. Election day is the
Tuesday after the first Monday of November. Enter at the close of the next
session and hold 252 sessions on the S&P 500 price index (10 bps); DEV is the
elections of 1950–1988, TEST those of 1990–2024. CPI: buy SPY at the close
before a release day (dates from ALFRED) and sell at that day's close; DEV is
1993–2012.

| Event | Period | n | Mean net | Positive | Any day | Edge over any day |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| After a midterm election | DEV 1950–1986 | 10 | +14.60% | 10 / 10 | +8.72% | +5.88% (+0.69…+11.12) |
| After a midterm election | TEST 1990–2022 | 9 | +14.42% | 9 / 9 | +9.89% | +4.53% (−1.32…+10.16) |
| After a presidential election | DEV 1952–1988 | 10 | +2.89% | 4 / 10 | +8.72% | −5.84% (−13.90…+3.16) |
| CPI release day, 10 bps | DEV 1993–2012 | 248 | +0.062% gross | 57.3% up | +0.037% | −0.075% (−0.225…+0.069) |
| CPI release day, 1 bp | DEV 1993–2012 | 248 | +0.062% gross | 57.3% up | +0.037% | +0.015% (−0.135…+0.159) |

The year after a midterm is the only rule in this research that passed its
development period, so it was run once on the held-out elections. The
direction held: nine of nine positive, and an edge of the same size. With nine
events the interval reaches below zero, so by the registered bar it is not
confirmed. Only five of the nine beat the any-day mean; the index is up over
80% of all 252-session holds in that period, so "positive every time" is weak
evidence on its own. Taken together the nineteen elections of 1950–2022
average +14.5% against +9.2% for any day (19 of 19 positive, worst +0.9%);
that pooled figure was not the registered test.

It also does not make a signal that beats the index. It says when to be in the
index, and a holder already is. Doubling exposure in those windows with the
actual 2× fund (SSO, five windows from 2006) lifts the account from 10.9% to
11.6% a year and deepens the worst drawdown from 55% to 59%; in the 2006 and
2010 windows SSO returned less than SPY itself. The post-presidential year and
CPI release days fail on their development periods and were not run further.
The 2026 midterm was on 2026-11-03, so the current window opened at the
2026-11-04 close.

## Conclusions

1. With delisted members restored, the stock rules still do not beat the index
   or the members they are chosen from. The earlier failure was not an
   artefact of the hand-picked universe.
2. The leader-pullback thesis, measured as a rank over ten years and roughly
   forty names a week, is +0.09% a week over SPY with an interval that
   spans zero. Nothing supports a claim that the shipped signal selects stocks.
3. Information beyond price does not rescue it. Insider buying and the
   earnings announcement premium, two of the better documented effects, do not
   clear the bar on index members in 2006–2015, and what they show sits in the
   2008–2009 rebound.
4. The one calendar effect with support, the year after a midterm election, is
   a reason to stay invested in the index through it, which the index holder
   already does. It was not confirmed on the held-out elections and does not
   select stocks.
5. The position stated in the entry research stands: capital in the index, the
   alerts as a screener.

## Reproduction

Scripts are in [`sp500-research-2026-11-27/`](sp500-research-2026-11-27/). From
the repository root, with `TIINGO_API_KEY` set:

```bash
(cd packages/core && bun ../../docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/fetch-yahoo.ts)
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/renames.mjs
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/tiingo-queue.mjs
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/fetch-tiingo.mjs \
  --list=data/strategy-validation/sp500-pit-20261127/tiingo-queue.txt --wait
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/weekly.mjs --coverage
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/weekly.mjs
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/insider-extract.mjs <zip directory>
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/cik-map.mjs <zip directory>
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/insider.mjs
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/earnings.mjs
(cd packages/core && bun ../../docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/fetch-index.ts)
node docs/validation/sp500-research-2026-11-27/events.mjs --final=midterm
```

`membership.csv`, `tiingo-supported.csv` (Tiingo's public ticker list) and
`wikipedia-constituents.wiki` (the raw constituent table) are downloaded into
the data directory first. Tiingo's free plan allows 50 requests an hour, so the
queue takes several hours.

The SEC files were fetched in a browser session, because sec.gov refuses
clients that do not declare a contact: the quarterly
`YYYYqN_form345.zip` insider data sets, and
`data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json` (with its older pages) for each
CIK in `cik-map.json`, reduced to `earnings-8k.json` as
`{ cik: { name, dates, accepted } }`. The derived files are kept under
`data/strategy-validation/sec-insider-20261127`. The CPI release dates
(`alfred.stlouisfed.org/release/downloaddates?rid=10&ff=txt`) and the index
series are under `data/strategy-validation/events-20261127`. `--final` was
used once, for the midterm rule.
